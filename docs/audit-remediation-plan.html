Findings

01

Your share tags and your search tags describe two different companies

P0 · blocking
meta name="description"
…agency, free audit…
Reads as: a service business staffed by people.
meta property="og:description"
autonomous engine that writes and publishes — you just approve it
Reads as: a software product that runs itself.

As reported by the audit — worth diffing against the live HTML before editing, since I can't reach ezweb.dev from here.

These aren't two angles on one company, they're two entity types: Organization/ProfessionalService versus SoftwareApplication. Models build an entity by finding the same claim repeated across sources; a page that makes both claims at once reinforces neither, and the site ends up cited for nothing in particular. Worse, publishes directly contradicts the on-page promise that nothing goes live without approval — so the one tag most likely to be quoted back at you undercuts your own trust argument.

The fix

Write one sentence that is true in both directions, then use that exact string everywhere. The offending word is publishes, not autonomous — keep the automation, drop the claim that it ships on its own. A draft to react to:

EZ Web builds and maintains law-firm websites with an AI engine that drafts and a human who reviews — nothing goes live until you approve it.

Then propagate it verbatim. Same string, no clever per-slot rewrites — the repetition is the signal:

meta description og:description twitter:description Organization.description homepage subhead LinkedIn company tagline GBP business description

Note the last two. Off-site profiles are where an entity gets corroborated; if they tell a third story you've reopened the same hole somewhere Google trusts more than your own markup.

02

The Organization node has no sameAs and no stable identity

P0

Missing sameAs is the headline, but the deeper problem is fragmentation: each page declares its own loose organization object, so there's no single node for a crawler to attach anything to. In this repo you can see both failure modes literally — sameAs: [] sitting empty at src/seo.tsx:39, and url: '/' on the homepage LocalBusiness at src/App.tsx:76, which is not a resolvable URL and does nothing. Whatever the live build looks like, assume the same shape until proven otherwise.

The fix

  • Declare the organization once, with a stable @id of https://ezweb.dev/#organization, and have every other node reference that @id as its provider, publisher, or author instead of restating the name.
  • Populate sameAs with the profiles you control: company LinkedIn, personal LinkedIn, GitHub org, and — once they exist — the GBP listing and any directory profiles from finding 05.
  • Make every url and @id absolute. Relative URLs in JSON-LD are a silent no-op.
  • Add founder as a Person node with its own @id and sameAs. A named human attached to the org is one of the few E-E-A-T signals available to a solo shop, and it does double duty against finding 06.

03

/for-law-firms/ has an FAQ with no FAQPage markup

P1

A visible FAQ with no markup is free structured data left on the floor — and Q&A pairs are disproportionately useful to answer engines, which lift them more or less intact. Add FAQPage with the question and answer text matching what's rendered on the page word for word; paraphrasing between the markup and the visible copy is the usual reason this gets ignored.

The fix

  • FAQPage — every visible Q&A, verbatim.
  • Service with serviceType: "Law firm website and search marketing", provider pointing at the #organization @id, and audience as { "@type": "Audience", "audienceType": "Law firms" }.
  • BreadcrumbList, since the page sits a level below home.
One correction to the audit

Don't add Attorney or LegalService markup. Those types describe an entity that practices law. You sell marketing to firms that do — declaring yourself a legal service is a false entity claim, and it would land in the same graph as your clients and their competitors. The audit called this a gap on the grounds that you "literally sell it on that page," but what the page sells is the audience, not the service type, and audience is the property that carries that. Given the whole thrust of the audit is entity clarity, this one would cut against it.

04

20 sitemap URLs with no lastmod

P1

Straightforward to fix, with one trap worth naming: a lastmod that always says "today" is worse than none at all. Crawlers learn within a few cycles that a given site's dates carry no information and stop weighting them — and this repo's generator does exactly that, stamping today on every static and service URL at build time (scripts/generate-sitemap.mjs:19-41) so a CSS tweak "modifies" all 24 pages. If the live generator was written the same way, the 20 missing dates and the 4 fake ones are the same bug.

The fix

  • Derive lastmod from the last commit that touched each page's own source or content file — git log -1 --format=%cI -- <file> at build time.
  • Or, if content is data-driven, carry an explicit updated field per entry and bump it when the copy actually changes.
  • Drop priority and changefreq entirely. Google ignores both, and they're noise you have to maintain.
05

No Google Business Profile and no third-party reviews

P2 · start now

The audit's sharpest point, and the only one that isn't a code change. You sell local search to law firms while having no local-search footprint and no independent proof of work — and your own /about/ page says so out loud. Every other fix here is a signal you assert about yourself; this is the only category of signal that comes from somewhere else, which is exactly why it carries more weight than all the markup combined.

The fix

  • Create and verify the GBP as a service-area business if there's no walk-in office. Category: Website designer or Internet marketing service. Business description gets the finding-01 sentence.
  • Ask your last handful of clients for reviews — Google first, then one directory (Clutch or UpCity read as credible in legal-adjacent buying).
  • Once the profiles exist, add them to sameAs from finding 02. That's the loop closing: the GBP corroborates the site, the site points back at the GBP.
  • Rewrite the /about/ admission once it's no longer true. Until then leave it — it's honest, and honest beats a claim you can't back.
Don't shortcut this with markup

Self-serving AggregateRating on your own Organization — ratings not tied to visible, independently sourced reviews — is ineligible for rich results and reads as manipulation. Earn the reviews, then mark up what genuinely exists on the page.

06

Every page has the same skeleton

P2 · ongoing

The audit's read is right and the diagnosis is precise: the sentences pass, the structure doesn't. Split headline, three cards, identical CTA stack, next page, repeat. Readers don't consciously clock the pattern — they just arrive at the third page having learned nothing new about how you work, and the "human reviewed" claim quietly stops being believable. Rewriting the prose won't touch this. The tell is the skeleton.

The fix

  • Break the triad. Use two, four, five, seven — whatever the content actually contains. Three-of-everything is the single loudest signal, and it's almost never what the material wants.
  • Give pages different spines. /for-law-firms/ should run as a narrative or a worked example. A service page could be a comparison table, or a "what we don't do" section. Blog posts want pull-quotes, a real screenshot, an inline number.
  • Vary the close. Different verbs, different placement, and at least one page with no CTA block at all. An identical stack in an identical position on every page is template residue.
  • Add what only you could write. A dated example, a named tool, a real dashboard screenshot, a number with its caveat attached. One specific detail does more than a page of well-edited generalities.
  • Audit unsourced stats. This repo's homepage carries "+210% avg. 6-mo organic" and "3.2x paid ROAS" with nothing behind them. Round, confident, unattributed numbers are their own AI tell — attribute them to a named engagement or cut them.
  • Put a byline on it. Name, photo, real bio, author schema pointing at the Person node from finding 02. Nothing says human-reviewed like a human whose name is attached.

Keep the line the audit singled out — "Schema barely moves citations, whatever the LinkedIn posts say." That's the register the rest of the site should be reaching for.

What I'd deliberately not do

✕

Attorney / LegalService markup. Wrong entity type, actively harmful to the clarity everything else here is buying. Covered in finding 03.

✕

AggregateRating before real reviews exist. Ineligible for rich results, and a bad look on a site whose pitch is trustworthy automation.

✕

Rewriting sentences to fix finding 06. The prose already passes. Paraphrasing burns a week and changes nothing, because the pattern is structural.

✕

Softening the entity sentence into something that covers everything. "Web development and digital marketing agency" is what the current repo says, and it's precisely the vagueness that leaves you uncited. Narrow beats broad for entity resolution.
